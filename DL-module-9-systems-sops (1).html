Systems, SOPs & Automation Thinking

High-performance organizations succeed because their talent operates inside systems that are predictable, documented, scalable, and adaptable. Systems thinking is a leadership skill that creates clarity, speed, and reliability across the entire client experience.

WHAT YOU'LL MASTER

  • Design and maintain systems that create predictable outcomes
  • Build SOPs that remove ambiguity from workflows
  • Identify bottlenecks and remove them proactively
  • Use automation thinking to reduce manual labor
  • Maintain documentation for consistent team execution
  • Improve internal clarity and cross-functional execution
  • Support Digital Leverage as it scales
SECTION 1

What a System Is and Why It Matters

A system is a repeatable sequence of actions that produces a consistent outcome with minimal variation. Without systems, teams operate on individual effort rather than operational intelligence. With systems, the organization grows without chaos.

✓Scale execution without burning out
✓Create predictable results for clients

Great AMs build systems that outlast them.

SECTION 2

The Systems Mindset for Account Managers

A systems mindset is the ability to see work as a flow of steps rather than a collection of tasks. This mindset transforms the AM from a reactive operator into a strategic builder. Systems thinking is not optional in high-performance operations. It is the operating logic of scale.

1

Predictive Awareness

You learn to anticipate breakdowns before they occur. This prevents fire drills and client frustration.

2

Structural Clarity

You see the underlying architecture of tasks and workflows. This helps you simplify complexity and reduce wasted effort.

3

Repetition Awareness

You notice which tasks repeat daily, weekly, or monthly. These tasks become the foundation of SOPs.

4

Documentation Discipline

You record decisions, steps, and standards clearly. This keeps the team aligned and reduces errors.

5

Efficiency Consciousness

You look for ways to reduce friction, streamline communication, and shorten execution time without sacrificing quality.

6

Automation Awareness

You identify patterns that can be systemized and delegated to tools—templates, scripts, or AI workflows.

SECTION 3

The Digital Leverage Execution System

This is the core operational engine behind the DL workflow. You must understand how each layer works together. When all six stages are systemized, content production becomes predictable and scalable.

1

Intake

Collecting insights from calls, messages, performance data, and client notes. This is where raw information enters the system.

2

Structuring

Translating insights into briefs, narratives, and campaign direction. Raw information becomes actionable strategy.

3

Assignment

Giving writers exact instructions with deadlines, tone notes, angles, and story material. Clarity here prevents revisions later.

4

Creation

The writer producing drafts inside a predictable process. The AM has already done the work to make this stage smooth.

5

Review

The AM analyzes drafts for voice, alignment, clarity, accuracy, and strategic fit. Quality control happens here.

6

Delivery

The AM presents the work to the client with confidence and clear context. The final handoff that builds trust.

SECTION 4

Building Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

SOPs transform institutional knowledge into written knowledge. This makes success repeatable and reduces dependence on individual memory. SOPs cannot be vague—they must be written so clearly that someone new to the team could follow them without asking questions.

1.

Purpose

Why this workflow exists. What problem does it solve?

2.

Trigger

What event starts the SOP. When does this workflow activate?

3.

Steps

The clear sequence from start to finish. No ambiguity.

4.

Ownership

Who is responsible for each step. Names, not roles.

5.

Tools

What tools are used. Links and access requirements.

6.

Inputs and Outputs

What information must come in and what must go out for the process to be complete.

7.

Edge Cases

What variations can occur and how to handle them.

8.

Documentation

Where the final record or asset is stored.

SECTION 5

The Eight Essential SOPs for Digital Leverage AMs

These are complete, executable procedures. Follow them exactly. Each SOP includes the trigger, every step, required outputs, and quality checks.

SOP 01

Client Call SOP

Trigger: Scheduled weekly/bi-weekly client call
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Call summary document + updated decision log

PRE-CALL (24 hours before)

  1. Review last week's performance data (engagement, velocity, AEPP trend)
  2. Identify 1-2 insights to share (what worked, what didn't, why)
  3. Review previous call notes for any open items or commitments
  4. Check client's recent LinkedIn/social activity for context
  5. Prepare 1 strategic recommendation with rationale
  6. Draft agenda and send to client 2+ hours before call
Agenda Template:
1. Quick wins from last week (2 min)
2. Performance insights (5 min)
3. Strategic recommendation (5 min)
4. Content direction for next week (10 min)
5. Open items / questions from you (5 min)
6. Next steps and commitments (3 min)

DURING CALL

  1. Start on time. If client is late 5+ min, send a "checking in" message
  2. Open with one specific positive observation (not generic praise)
  3. Present data as patterns, not individual posts
  4. State your recommendation clearly: "My suggestion is X because Y"
  5. Ask open questions: "What's on your mind?" "Anything shifting in your business?"
  6. Listen for emotional signals—note tone changes, hesitations, concerns
  7. Capture all decisions and action items in real-time notes
  8. Verbally confirm next steps before ending: "So to confirm, we're doing X, Y, Z"

POST-CALL (within 2 hours)

  1. Write call summary (use template below)
  2. Send summary to client via preferred channel
  3. Update decision log with any strategic shifts
  4. Create tasks for any commitments you made
  5. Brief writer if direction changed
  6. Flag any churn signals to your manager if detected
Call Summary Template:
CALL SUMMARY — [Client Name] — [Date]

KEY DECISIONS:
• [Decision 1]
• [Decision 2]

CONTENT DIRECTION:
• Angles to prioritize: [X, Y]
• Angles to reduce: [Z]
• Tone notes: [any adjustments]

ACTION ITEMS:
• [AM] [Task] — Due [Date]
• [Client] [Task] — Due [Date]

NEXT CALL: [Date/Time]
SOP 02

Briefing SOP

Trigger: New content cycle begins OR strategic direction changes
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Complete brief document ready for writer handoff

BRIEF CREATION STEPS

  1. Open brief template (never start from blank)
  2. Fill in client context section (who they are, what they do, who they serve)
  3. Define the campaign objective in ONE sentence
  4. List 3-5 angles with specific definitions and examples
  5. Specify tone: pick from [Authoritative / Conversational / Vulnerable / Aspirational / Tactical]
  6. Add "What Success Looks Like" — link to 1-2 posts that hit the mark
  7. Add "What to Avoid" — specific patterns or topics to skip
  8. Include raw story materials (quotes, anecdotes, insights from calls)
  9. Set posting cadence and deadlines
  10. Run quality check before sending
Brief Template — Required Fields:
CLIENT: [Name]
CAMPAIGN PERIOD: [Date range]

1. OBJECTIVE (one sentence):
   What is this content trying to accomplish?

2. PRIMARY NARRATIVE:
   What overarching story are we telling about this client?

3. ANGLES (3-5):
   • Angle 1: [Name] — [Definition] — [Example topic]
   • Angle 2: [Name] — [Definition] — [Example topic]
   • Angle 3: [Name] — [Definition] — [Example topic]

4. TONE:
   [Select one + any modifiers]

5. WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:
   [Link to example post that nailed it]
   Why it worked: [1-2 sentences]

6. WHAT TO AVOID:
   • [Specific thing to skip]
   • [Another thing to skip]

7. STORY MATERIALS:
   • [Quote from client]
   • [Anecdote from call]
   • [Data point to reference]

8. POSTING CADENCE:
   [X posts per week] — Deadlines: [dates]

9. PERFORMANCE CONTEXT:
   What's working: [angle/topic]
   What's underperforming: [angle/topic]

QUALITY CHECK (before sending)

SOP 03

Writer Handoff SOP

Trigger: Brief is complete and ready for execution
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Writer has everything needed to execute without questions

HANDOFF STEPS

  1. Confirm brief passes quality check (see SOP 02)
  2. Assign writer in project management tool
  3. Send handoff message (use template below)
  4. Set deadline with 24-hour buffer before client delivery date
  5. Ask: "Does anything need clarification before you start?"
  6. Respond to any questions within 4 hours
  7. Document assignment in client folder
Handoff Message Template:
Hey [Writer],

New brief for [Client Name] — [X posts] due [Date].

QUICK CONTEXT:
[1-2 sentences on what's happening with this client right now]

THE ASK:
[Specific deliverable: e.g., "3 LinkedIn posts, 800-1200 words each"]

KEY DIRECTION:
• Lead with [angle]
• Tone should feel [X]
• Avoid [specific thing]

WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE:
[Link to example] — this worked because [reason]

MATERIALS:
[Link to brief with all story materials]

DEADLINE: [Date, Time]

Let me know if anything is unclear before you dive in.

HANDOFF QUALITY CHECK

SOP 04

Content Review SOP

Trigger: Writer submits draft
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Approved content OR specific revision requests

REVIEW STEPS

  1. Read the full draft without editing first (get the overall feel)
  2. Check against brief: Does it match the objective?
  3. Check voice: Does it sound like the client?
  4. Check angle: Is the angle clear and consistent?
  5. Check hook: Would you stop scrolling for this?
  6. Check accuracy: Any claims that need verification?
  7. Check CTA: Is there a clear next step or takeaway?
  8. Make decision: Approve / Minor edits / Major revision
  9. Document your feedback using the Feedback SOP

REVIEW CHECKLIST

DECISION CRITERIA

✓ APPROVE

Passes all checklist items. Ready for client or scheduling.

↻ MINOR EDITS

1-3 small fixes. You can make them yourself or send back with specific notes.

⟳ MAJOR REVISION

Misses objective, voice, or angle. Requires rewrite. Use Feedback SOP.

SOP 05

Feedback SOP

Trigger: Content needs revision OR performance review with writer
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Clear, actionable feedback that improves future work

FEEDBACK PRINCIPLES

  • Specific, not vague: "The hook needs more tension" not "Make it better"
  • Actionable: Tell them what to do, not just what's wrong
  • Respectful: Critique the work, not the person
  • Prioritized: Lead with the most important issue
  • Contextual: Explain why it matters for this client

FEEDBACK STEPS

  1. Start with one thing that worked (specific, genuine)
  2. State the primary issue clearly
  3. Explain WHY it's an issue (connect to client/audience)
  4. Give specific direction on how to fix it
  5. Provide an example if possible
  6. Prioritize: if multiple issues, number them by importance
  7. End with confidence: "Once you adjust X, this will be strong"
Feedback Message Template:
Hey [Writer],

Thanks for this draft. [One specific thing that worked.]

A few adjustments needed:

1. [MOST IMPORTANT] [Specific issue]
   → Why it matters: [context]
   → Fix: [specific action]

2. [Issue #2]
   → Fix: [specific action]

3. [Issue #3 if needed]
   → Fix: [specific action]

Example of what I'm looking for: [link or quote]

Once you nail #1, this will be in great shape. Let me know if
you want to talk through any of this.

FEEDBACK DON'TS

  • ❌ "This doesn't work" (vague)
  • ❌ "Make it more engaging" (not actionable)
  • ❌ "The client won't like this" (unhelpful)
  • ❌ Rewriting the entire piece yourself (doesn't teach)
  • ❌ Sending 10+ comments at once (overwhelming)
  • ❌ Giving feedback publicly (demotivating)
SOP 06

Client Delivery SOP

Trigger: Content is approved and ready for client review
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Client receives content with context; approves or provides feedback

DELIVERY STEPS

  1. Final quality check: re-read content one more time
  2. Prepare delivery message (use template below)
  3. Include strategic context: why this content, why this angle
  4. Set clear expectation for response: "Let me know by [date]"
  5. Send via client's preferred channel
  6. If no response in 24 hours, send gentle follow-up
  7. Document approval in client folder
  8. Schedule content once approved
Delivery Message Template:
Hey [Client],

Here's this week's content — [X posts] ready for your review.

[Link to content]

QUICK CONTEXT:
Based on [insight from last call / performance data], we're
leading with [angle] this week. The goal is to [specific objective].

Post 1: [Brief description — why we're doing this]
Post 2: [Brief description]
Post 3: [Brief description]

Let me know if these are good to go, or if you'd like any
adjustments. Ideally by [date] so we stay on schedule.

Talk soon.

HANDLING CLIENT FEEDBACK

  • Minor edits: Make them yourself, confirm with client, move forward
  • Subjective preference: Accommodate if reasonable; push back gently if it hurts strategy
  • Major direction change: Get on a call to realign; don't guess via text
  • No response: Follow up at 24h and 48h. If still nothing, flag for weekly call.
SOP 07

Performance Review SOP

Trigger: Weekly (every Monday) + Monthly (first week of month)
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Updated dashboard + 1-2 insights + recommended action

WEEKLY REVIEW STEPS (Every Monday)

  1. Pull last week's post data: impressions, engagement, velocity
  2. Calculate AEPP (Average Engagement Per Post) for the week
  3. Compare to 4-week baseline: up, down, or flat?
  4. Identify top performer: What angle? What hook? What made it work?
  5. Identify underperformer: What variable might explain it?
  6. Update dashboard with new data
  7. Write 1 insight sentence: "This week we learned that..."
  8. Decide: Reinforce / Rotate / Retire any angles?
  9. Prep insight for client call
Weekly Review Template:
WEEK OF: [Date]
CLIENT: [Name]

POSTS THIS WEEK: [#]
TOTAL ENGAGEMENT: [#]
AEPP: [#] (vs. baseline: [+/-X%])

TOP PERFORMER:
• Post: [title/topic]
• Engagement: [#]
• Why it worked: [1 sentence]

UNDERPERFORMER:
• Post: [title/topic]
• Engagement: [#]
• Hypothesis: [1 sentence]

INSIGHT:
"This week we learned that [specific observation]."

RECOMMENDED ACTION:
[ ] Reinforce [angle] — it's working
[ ] Rotate away from [angle] — underperforming
[ ] Test [new variable] next week

MONTHLY REVIEW STEPS (First Monday of month)

  1. Complete weekly review first
  2. Pull 30-day data: total reach, total engagement, follower growth
  3. Calculate monthly AEPP trend: improving or declining?
  4. Review all angles used: rank by performance
  5. Identify narrative patterns: what themes resonate?
  6. Update Narrative Resonance Index (NRI)
  7. Check Client Behavior Score: any warning signs?
  8. Write monthly summary for client (or internal review)
  9. Recommend strategic direction for next month
SOP 08

Risk Escalation SOP

Trigger: Warning signals detected (see list below)
Owner: Account Manager
Output: Early intervention OR escalation to leadership

WARNING SIGNALS (Monitor weekly)

Performance Signals
  • 3+ posts below baseline in a row
  • AEPP declining for 2+ weeks
  • Velocity dropping consistently
  • Two core angles failing
Behavioral Signals
  • Shorter client messages
  • Slower response times
  • Cancelled or shortened calls
  • Increased scrutiny on small details
  • Less future-oriented language
  • Reduced emotional warmth

ESCALATION LEVELS

LEVEL 1: Monitor

Trigger: 1-2 signals appear

Action: Note in client file. Increase attention. No outreach yet.

LEVEL 2: Proactive Outreach

Trigger: 3+ signals OR 2 signals persist for 2 weeks

Action: Reach out to client outside normal rhythm. Use script below.

LEVEL 3: Leadership Escalation

Trigger: Client expresses doubt, mentions leaving, or goes silent for 7+ days

Action: Notify your manager immediately. Prepare situation summary.

Level 2 Outreach Script:
"Hey [Client], I wanted to check in outside our usual rhythm.
I've been thinking about [their goals/situation] and want to
make sure we're still aligned on direction.

Do you have 15 minutes this week to sync? I have a few ideas
I'd love to run by you."
Level 3 Escalation Template (for your manager):
RISK ALERT: [Client Name]

SIGNALS DETECTED:
• [Signal 1]
• [Signal 2]
• [Signal 3]

TIMELINE:
• [When signals started]
• [What actions I've taken]
• [Current status]

MY ASSESSMENT:
[1-2 sentences on what you think is happening]

RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP:
[What you think we should do]

URGENCY: [High / Medium]

POST-INTERVENTION

  1. Document the intervention and outcome in client file
  2. Update Client Behavior Score
  3. Schedule closer check-ins for next 2-4 weeks
  4. If issue resolved: note what worked for future reference
  5. If issue persists: escalate to Level 3
SECTION 6

Automation Thinking for Account Managers

Automation thinking is not about coding. It's about recognizing patterns that can be systemized and delegated to tools. Automation frees mental bandwidth so you can focus on strategy and client relationships.

🔄 Identification of Repetitive Tasks

Any task done more than three times per week is a candidate for automation. Track your work for patterns.

📝 Template Development

Templates remove guesswork and reduce cognitive load for both you and the team.

  • Brief templates
  • Call agenda templates
  • Feedback frameworks
  • Client update formats

🤖 AI-Supported Workflows

AI tools can assist with routine processing tasks:

  • Cleaning call notes
  • Generating summaries
  • Analyzing performance data
  • Drafting briefs
  • Updating documentation
  • Organizing insights

⚡ Procedural Shortcuts

Accelerate routine work without automation tools:

  • Checklists
  • Macros
  • Saved responses
  • Shared documents

🎯 Automation Triggers

Set up automatic next steps when conditions are met:

  • Brief created → auto-create writer assignment
  • Call completed → auto-generate summary
  • Data updated → flag outliers for AM
  • Deadline approaching → send reminder

SECTION 8

Improvement Systems and Feedback Loops

Systems must evolve. DL uses iterative improvement cycles to keep workflows healthy. Organizations grow from structured reflection, not from accidental improvement.

Weekly

AMs review what went well and what broke. Quick identification of friction points and immediate fixes.

Monthly

AMs update SOPs based on new insights, client patterns, or performance data. Documentation stays current.

Quarterly

Leadership and AMs review larger system adjustments needed for scale. Strategic evolution of workflows.

Continuous

Feedback loops: Writers give feedback on briefs. AMs give feedback on workflow. Leadership gives feedback on alignment.

SECTION 9

System Failure Scenarios and How to Resolve Them

These scenarios build real operational intelligence. Practice diagnosing system failures and implementing fixes.

01

Revisions Spiral Out of Control

A piece of content is now on its fourth revision. The client keeps requesting changes. The writer is frustrated. Time is being wasted.

EXPERT APPROACH

Diagnosis: Track back to the brief. Was the objective clear? Was the tone specified? Did the brief include "what success looks like" and "what to avoid"? Most revision spirals trace back to unclear briefing.

System fix: Update the Briefing SOP to require explicit sign-off on these fields before work begins. Add a pre-flight checklist: "Does the brief answer: What's the goal? What's the tone? What's the angle? What does good look like?"

Immediate action: Get on a call with the client to align on exactly what they want. Document it. Then give the writer a clean, specific brief with that clarity.

02

Client Misses Multiple Deadlines

The client is supposed to provide story materials and approve briefs on a schedule. They keep missing their deadlines, which pushes everything back. The team is scrambling.

EXPERT APPROACH

Conversation: "I want to make sure we're set up for success. The last few weeks, we've had to compress timelines because approvals came in late. That puts pressure on the writing team and sometimes affects quality."

Reset: "Can we agree on a rhythm where I send you materials by [day], and you get back to me by [day]? That gives us the buffer we need to deliver at the level you expect."

System fix: Build client deadlines into the SOP with automatic reminders. Document the agreement. If the pattern continues, the documentation becomes your evidence for a larger conversation about expectations.

03

Writer Becomes Confused

A writer messages you saying they don't understand what you're asking for. They've read the brief but can't figure out the direction. This is the second time this week.

EXPERT APPROACH

Immediate fix: Get on a quick call or voice note. Walk them through: "Here's the goal. Here's the emotional tone. Here's an example of something that hit the mark. Here's what to avoid."

System fix: Review your briefs. Are you assuming context that the writer doesn't have? Update the Writer Handoff SOP to require: (1) One-sentence objective, (2) Specific tone guidance, (3) Example content that worked, (4) What to avoid.

Feedback loop: Ask the writer: "What would have made this clearer?" Use their answer to improve your template.

04

Performance Dips Unexpectedly

Content has been performing well for two months. Suddenly, the last three posts are significantly below baseline. The client hasn't noticed yet, but you have.

EXPERT APPROACH

Step 1 - Isolate: What do the three underperforming posts have in common? Same angle? Same hook structure? Same posting time? Same topic category?

Step 2 - Compare: How do they differ from the last three posts that performed well?

Step 3 - Hypothesize: If all three underperformers used tactical how-to content while strong performers were story-driven, you have your signal.

Step 4 - Act: Update the campaign brief to rotate away from the underperforming angle. Document your hypothesis and the test you're running.

05

A Bottleneck Appears in the Workflow

Content is getting stuck somewhere in the process. You're not sure where. Deadlines are slipping, but no one can point to the exact problem.

EXPERT APPROACH

Step 1 - Map: Write out the full workflow: Intake → Structuring → Assignment → Creation → Review → Delivery. Note the expected time at each stage.

Step 2 - Measure: Track the last 5 pieces of content. Where did they spend the most time? Where did delays occur?

Step 3 - Identify: The bottleneck is usually where work piles up waiting for the next step. Is it waiting for your review? Waiting for client approval? Waiting for writer capacity?

Step 4 - Fix: If review is the bottleneck, batch your reviews at a set time. If client approval is slow, add earlier reminders. If writer capacity is the issue, redistribute or adjust scope.
