MODULE 7

Data, Dashboards & Decision Making

Account Managers must make decisions based on evidence, not instinct. Data improves clarity. Data reduces uncertainty. Data sharpens strategy. This is not a module about spreadsheets. This is a module about becoming a strategic thinker.

WHAT YOU'LL MASTER

  • Understand which metrics actually matter for personal-brand clients
  • Build and read dashboards that reflect client health
  • Interpret performance patterns with accuracy and logic
  • Detect churn risk before it becomes visible
  • Use analytic thinking to guide content strategy
  • Translate data into clear recommendations
  • Turn weekly numbers into narrative insights
  • Present data in a way that clients trust
  • Make decisions calmly without overreacting to outlier posts
SECTION 1

The Purpose of Data for an Account Manager

Data is not about numbers. Data is about meaning-making. Data elevates the AM from a "content coordinator" to a strategic advisor.

1

Data Builds Trust

Clients trust explanations backed by evidence. When you can show them why something works, they believe your recommendations.

2

Data Reduces Emotional Volatility

When clients panic over performance dips, you stabilize them with facts. Data is your anchor in emotional conversations.

3

Data Guides Strategic Decisions

You use numbers to identify what angles are working, what narratives resonate, and what should change.

4

Data Reveals Hidden Risks

You catch drops in engagement, pacing, or sentiment before the client notices. Early detection prevents problems.

5

Data Allows You to Advocate Internally

You can protect writers and guide leadership with objective reasoning. Data makes your case for you.

SECTION 2

The Six Core Metrics Every DL AM Must Track

There are hundreds of metrics across social platforms. Most do not matter. Digital Leverage uses six metrics that directly correlate with client satisfaction, narrative health, and long-term retention.

01

Engagement Velocity

EV

The speed at which a post gathers engagement within the first 60 minutes and 24 hours. Velocity reveals whether the angle has resonance with the algorithm and audience.

02

Average Engagement Per Post

AEPP

Your baseline. It shows long-term audience warmth. If AEPP rises, narrative strength is increasing. If it drops, investigate.

03

Content Stability Score

CSS

Reflects consistency across posts. Clients trust stable performance more than sporadic spikes. Consistency signals reliability.

04

Narrative Resonance Index

NRI

Tracks which angles perform above baseline consistently over time. This informs campaign direction and angle rotation decisions.

05

Client Behavior Score

CBS

Tracks client-side signals that correlate with satisfaction. Often more predictive of churn than platform metrics.

  • Call attendance
  • Message responsiveness
  • Tone patterns
  • Revisions requested
  • Openness to ideas
06

Churn Risk Indicator

CRI

A composite score based on multiple signals. Helps the AM intervene early before problems become irreversible.

  • Velocity trends
  • AEPP movement
  • CBS patterns
  • Call sentiment
  • Client language patterns

This is the analytic backbone of the AM role. Master these six metrics and you'll see patterns others miss.

SECTION 3

The DL Data Dashboard Structure

Every AM should maintain a dashboard with four sections. This creates a complete picture of account health.

1. Weekly Performance Summary

Shows post-by-post performance against expectations:

  • Each post with velocity score
  • Engagement vs. baseline
  • Angle used
  • Emotional trigger used
  • Writer notes

2. Trend Charts

Prevents overreacting to individual posts:

  • 2-week trend
  • 30-day trend
  • Angle-specific performance
  • Narrative-specific performance

3. Client Health Snapshot

Helps detect relationship risks early:

  • Call attendance
  • Responsiveness patterns
  • Tone patterns
  • Feedback patterns
  • Satisfaction indicators

4. AM Decision Log

Creates institutional memory:

  • Recommendations made
  • Pivots and adjustments
  • Client preferences
  • Observed audience shifts
  • Lessons learned
SECTION 4

Interpreting Data: A Decision-Making Framework

Most AMs fail at data not because they cannot measure it, but because they cannot interpret it. Digital Leverage uses a four-step interpretation process that prevents emotional decision-making.

1

Identify the Pattern

Look for repeated behavior across multiple posts. One post tells you nothing. Three to five posts tell you a trend.

2

Identify the Variable

Determine what changed. You must isolate the variable to understand causation.

Angle Tone Story Hook Time Emotion CTA Topic Platform dynamics
3

Identify the Meaning

Ask: "What does this pattern tell me about the audience's interest and the client's narrative direction?" Connect data to strategy.

4

Make the Recommendation

Decide on a clear action, then brief the writer clearly.

Reinforce Rotate Retire Refine Experiment Pivot
SECTION 5

Detecting Churn Risk Using Data + Behavioral Signals

Churn is rarely caused by performance alone. It is caused by patterns of disengagement. The AM must monitor both quantitative platform metrics and qualitative behavioral metrics. The combination produces the Churn Risk Indicator (CRI).

Quantitative Churn Risk Signals

  • Three posts in a row below baseline
  • Declining velocity trends
  • Decreasing average engagement over 30 days
  • Failure of two core angles
  • Increasing CTA dropoff

Qualitative Churn Risk Signals

  • Shorter client messages
  • Slower response times
  • Cancelled calls
  • Increased scrutiny of small issues
  • Reduced emotional warmth
  • Less future-oriented conversation
  • Language indicating doubt or frustration

The CRI gives the AM a factual basis for early intervention. You don't wait for the client to announce they're leaving. You see the signals and act.

SECTION 6

How to Discuss Data with Clients

Every AM must be skilled in presenting data in a way that reduces anxiety, builds confidence, increases trust, clarifies direction, and inspires future movement. Clients do not want spreadsheets. Clients want meaning. The AM's job is not to show data. The AM's job is to make data useful.

1.

Begin with the narrative: Start with insight, not numbers.

"Here's what we learned this week..."
2.

Show the pattern, not the numbers: Humans understand stories, not statistics.

"Across the last three posts, we noticed the audience responding strongest when you shared personal lessons..."
3.

Interpret the insight: Tell them what it means for their goals.

"This tells us your audience wants to see more of your journey, not just your advice..."
4.

Recommend a direction: Give them a clear path forward.

"My suggestion is that we lean into these experience-based angles for the next two weeks..."
5.

Show the path forward: Connect this week to next week.

"Here's what we'll test: two posts using this angle, one using your framework approach, so we can compare..."
SECTION 7

The Data-Driven Weekly Workflow

Below is the weekly routine of a data-literate DL AM. This routine builds consistency into your decision-making.

Monday

Review last week's performance. Update the dashboard. Identify one insight that will guide this week's work.

Tuesday

Work with the writer to integrate those insights into briefs. Translate data into creative direction.

Wednesday

Prepare client call with three elements:

  • One insight
  • One adjustment
  • One directional recommendation
Thursday

Run the call, document decisions, update the decision log. Capture any shifts in client priorities or concerns.

Friday

Review platform analytics again. Write a weekly narrative summary for yourself. Note any early churn risk indicators.

SECTION 8

The Analytic Thinking Mindset

Data literacy is not about platforms. It is about how you think. You do not need to be a data scientist. You need to think with structure.

🔍Avoid assumptions
😌Separate emotion from observation
📊Compare performance to baselines

SECTION 9

Data Scenarios and Practice Drills

Practice applying the frameworks you've learned to real situations.

01

The Misleading Spike

A post goes viral. The client celebrates. But when you dig into the comments, the sentiment is mixed—some negative, some off-topic. The engagement numbers look great, but something feels off.

EXPERT APPROACH

Acknowledge the win: "This got significant reach—that's great visibility." Then add context: "Let's look at what kind of engagement it drove. The comments show a mix of reactions. Some people engaged with the controversy rather than the insight."

Interpret: "High reach + mixed sentiment often means we hit a nerve, but not necessarily in a way that builds authority. The audience grew, but we want to make sure the right people are paying attention."

Recommend: "Let's follow this with a post that reinforces your core positioning—something that attracts the audience you actually want to work with."

02

The Downward Trend

Three posts in a row have declined in performance. Engagement is down 25% from the baseline. The client hasn't noticed yet, but you have.

EXPERT APPROACH

First, isolate: What do the three posts have in common? Same angle? Same tone? Same hook structure? Same posting time? Compare them to the last three posts that performed well.

Pattern identification: Let's say all three underperformers were tactical/how-to content, while the strong performers were personal stories. That's your signal.

Recommendation: "The data suggests your audience is responding more to experience-based content than tactical advice right now. Let's test this: next week, we'll run two story-driven posts and one tactical post to see if the pattern holds."

03

The Client Panic

A client messages urgently: "Engagement is way down this week. What's going on? Are we doing something wrong?" Their tone is anxious. You need to respond in a way that calms them while being honest.

EXPERT APPROACH

Insight 1 - Context: "I looked at the data. This week's posts are down about 15% from your baseline—but let me give you context. Your 30-day trend is still positive. One week doesn't tell us much."

Insight 2 - Variable: "The two posts that underperformed both used the same hook structure. That's likely the issue, not the content itself. Your previous three posts with different hooks performed above baseline."

Insight 3 - Action: "Here's what I'm doing: adjusting the hook approach for next week's content. We'll test two variations. If they perform better, we've found the issue. If not, we'll look at other variables."

04

The Narrative Misfire

You shifted to a new angle two weeks ago based on client input. It's underperformed consistently. Four posts, all below baseline. The angle isn't resonating.

EXPERT APPROACH

Be direct with the data: "I've reviewed the last two weeks. The new angle has underperformed in all four posts—velocity is down, engagement is below baseline. The data is clear: this angle isn't connecting with your current audience."

Diagnose: "It could be that this topic is important to you but not to your audience—or it could be that we need to approach it differently. The topic might work, but the framing isn't landing."

Guide rotation: "Here's my recommendation: let's return to the angles that were working—[specific angles]—for the next two weeks. If you still want to explore this topic, we can try a different approach later, maybe embedding it within a story rather than leading with it."

05

Early Churn Indicators

Behavioral patterns have shifted. The client's messages are shorter. They postponed the last call. Engagement is flat. Nothing is dramatically wrong, but something feels different.

EXPERT APPROACH

Document what you're seeing: (1) Shorter messages—3 weeks of declining length, (2) Postponed call—first time in 4 months, (3) Flat engagement—not bad, but no growth. CBS is declining even though content metrics are stable.

Don't wait. Reach out: "Hey [name], I wanted to check in outside our normal rhythm. I've been thinking about your goals for Q1 and want to make sure we're still aligned. Do you have 15 minutes this week to talk through what's on your mind?"

On the call: "Before we talk about content, I want to make sure I understand where you're at. How are you feeling about things overall?" Then listen. Surface the real concern before it becomes a churn conversation.
