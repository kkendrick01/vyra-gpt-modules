SECTION 1

What "Leadership" Means Inside Digital Leverage

Leadership is not a job title. It's a pattern of behavior that affects outcomes for clients, team, and company. Inside DL, leadership shows up in five concrete ways—and you're already building these muscles.

🎯

Owning Outcomes

You take responsibility for results. You don't blame circumstances or other people. You focus on what you can influence—and you influence more than you think.

📈

Raising Standards

You notice weak spots and improve them. You push your own work and the team's work to a higher level of clarity and quality. You don't settle.

💡

Creating Clarity

You simplify complexity. You give people simple, clear, actionable direction. You reduce confusion—for clients, for writers, for everyone around you.

⚙️

Thinking in Systems

You see patterns, workflows, and leverage. You don't just solve individual problems—you fix the process that produced the problem.

🌱

Building People

You help writers, other AMs, and future hires grow. You coach, encourage, and give pointed feedback that improves performance. You make others better.

Here's what you need to know: Leadership inside DL is visible, trackable, and practical. It's not abstract. It's not about charisma or politics. It's about doing the things that matter, consistently, with care.

And you've already started.

SECTION 2

Leadership Competencies for the AM → VP Path

This section translates "leadership" into specific competencies. Each one builds on modules you've already completed. You're not starting from zero—you're building on a foundation you've already laid.

Strategic Thinking

You mentor and elevate others:

  • Incoming staff

You share what works. You offer frameworks. You help them avoid mistakes you've already solved.

Self-Management

You manage your own time, energy, emotional regulation, boundaries, and focus. Leadership is unsustainable without self-management. Burned out people don't lead well.

This is the foundation that makes everything else possible.

SECTION 4

Internal Leadership: Working with Mel, Thomas, and the Core Team

Leadership is relational. You increase your influence by how you communicate with the people who steer DL. Here's how to show up in a way that builds trust and respect.

Communicating with Mel and Operations Leadership

What Mel cares about:

Stability, efficiency, client experience, team morale, process health

You lead well upward when you:
  • Summarize issues clearly and concisely
  • Bring proposed solutions, not just problems
  • Show how your idea affects time, cost, and complexity
  • Show awareness of impact on writers and other AMs
  • Follow through on what you commit to

Communicating with Thomas and Strategy Leadership

What Thomas cares about:

Brand, proof, narrative direction, high-leverage outcomes, category impact

You lead well upward when you:
  • Bring strong client stories and proof
  • Flag clients who are becoming case study material
  • Propose campaigns that support DL's public narrative
  • Stay calm when things spike or dip—ground perspective in data

Building Trust Inside Leadership Circles

Trust comes from repeated patterns. You build it by:

  • Doing what you say you will do
  • Owning mistakes without dramatizing them
  • Giving honest input even when it's uncomfortable
  • Keeping client conversations aligned with DL values
  • Showing care for the team, not only for your own accounts

Over time, this makes you someone leadership relies on.

SECTION 5

Leading Sideways: Peer and Team Leadership

Leadership isn't only "upward." It's also lateral. The way you show up with peers shapes culture and builds your reputation as someone worth following.

Be the AM Others Go To

You become a reference point when you:

  • Share your frameworks openly
  • Help troubleshoot difficult client moments
  • Offer to review a complex call structure
  • Suggest SOP improvements that benefit everyone

Set Visible Standards

Peers watch what you do, not what you say. You lead when you:

  • Show up prepared for every call
  • Send clean, complete briefs
  • Write precise internal comments
  • Stay composed when a client is upset
  • Handle performance dips with logic and structure

Protect Culture Through Your Behavior

You protect culture when you:

  • Speak respectfully about clients and colleagues
  • Avoid gossip
  • Bring issues to the right forum instead of venting sideways
  • Reinforce shared standards in a calm way
  • Celebrate wins and give credit publicly

A strong culture multiplies the effect of every system you built in Module 9.

SECTION 6

Decision-Making at Leadership Level

Module 7 covered data-driven decision making. Module 10 adds leadership context. Leaders run decisions through four filters—especially when under time pressure or emotional load.

Client Impact

How will this choice affect trust, satisfaction, and long-term relationship?

Team Impact

How will this affect workload, morale, and focus?

Business Impact

How will this affect revenue, margins, and risk?

Reputation Impact

How will this affect DL's brand in the market?

A leadership-level AM learns to pause and run decisions through these four filters. The extra 30 seconds of reflection prevents hours of cleanup later.

You already have the instincts. This framework makes them systematic.

SECTION 7

Mistakes That Block Leadership Trajectory

Sometimes it's easier to see what to avoid than what to chase. These patterns slow or stop leadership growth. Recognizing them is the first step to choosing a different path.

⚠️

Chronic defensiveness when given feedback

⚠️

Emotional reactivity in front of the team or clients

⚠️

Habit of escalating everything upward instead of owning solutions

⚠️

Disorganized personal workflow that creates avoidable emergencies

⚠️

Lack of documentation, so insights stay trapped in your head

⚠️

Protecting your accounts instead of sharing knowledge

⚠️

Speaking vaguely about problems instead of naming specifics

None of these are character flaws. They're habits. And habits can be changed.

You're already aware enough to read this list with honesty. That awareness is the foundation of growth.

SECTION 8

The 6-Month Leadership Development Plan

This is a concrete trajectory for someone who wants to move from high-performing AM toward VP of Client Strategy or VP of CS. Use this as your roadmap.

MONTHS 1–2
Stabilize Mastery of Your Current Accounts
  • Run every call with clear agendas and summaries
  • Improve briefs until writers consistently say they're clear
  • Document one SOP that removes a recurring friction point
  • Create one simple dashboard that tracks health for your accounts
  • Have one honest conversation with leadership about how they see your role today
MONTHS 3–4
Start Behaving Like a Team-Level Leader
  • Offer help to at least one other AM with a difficult account situation
  • Run a short internal Loom or session where you share a framework that works for you
  • Propose one system improvement that affects more than just your clients
  • Bring two strong client proof stories to leadership that could become case studies
  • Ask for feedback on where they see you acting like a leader—and where there are gaps
MONTHS 5–6
Step Into Visible Leadership Experiments
  • Take temporary lead on a small internal initiative that improves process or quality
  • Mentor a new writer or AM and track their progress over those months
  • Help refine or create one training asset for future hires
  • Present a short account portfolio review to leadership—framed in data, risk, and opportunity
  • Design a personal "Leadership OS" document that describes how you operate, what you own, and how you want to grow

If you execute this plan consistently, you don't need to request leadership status.

You'll already be acting in alignment with it. The title will follow the behavior.
